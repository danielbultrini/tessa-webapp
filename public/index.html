TESSA

Encode pixel art on an IBM chip, then read it back. Simulations use the chip's saved calibration; you can also send the job to IBM hardware.

evenly spaced from Z (0°) to X (90°)

The key and CRN go to this app's server only to submit and check the job. They are not stored, and the page forgets them when you close it. Hardware runs the whole circuit at once, so a job can wait in IBM's queue for a long time.

Hover over a pixel or a data qubit to see where it lives.

Noise pulls the read-back toward the middle of its range. Dynamic range recovery (DRR) stretches it back, using only a few numbers from your image, such as its minimum and maximum, or its mean and spread. TESSA tries every method and shows the one that comes closest to your image. Choosing it needs your original image, which a real use without the original would not have. Hue is never stretched, because it wraps around.
